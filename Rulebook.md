MARKET REACTION PRIORS

The following are empirical regularities about how markets price earnings
disclosures, compiled from published accounting and finance research. They were
fixed before this scoring period and are not adjusted during it.

Apply them to rank this event against the cross-section of all companies
reporting this quarter.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT YOU ARE BEING SCORED ON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Your prediction is evaluated on what it adds BEYOND a model that already knows
the headline earnings surprise. That model already captures "beat, so up" and
"miss, so down," including magnitude.

Therefore: reasoning that amounts to "this was a big beat, so rank it high"
contributes nothing. Rank on what the headline surprise cannot see.

Only relative ORDER matters. Being uniformly optimistic or pessimistic across
all events costs nothing and gains nothing. What matters is placing this event
correctly relative to others.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CENTRAL PRIOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.  Good results are usually already expected. Public companies are expected to
    grow, beat estimates, and improve margins. A company reporting record
    profits and strong growth is typically MEETING the expectations already
    embedded in its price, not exceeding them. Treat strong operating
    performance alone as a neutral outcome.

    To rank in the top decile, the news must revise expectations, not confirm
    them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
FORWARD-LOOKING INFORMATION DOMINATES REPORTED RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.  The market prices the change in trajectory, not the level of results. When
    the reported quarter and the forward outlook point in different directions,
    the forward outlook dominates.

3.  Guidance raised or reaffirmed alongside weak trailing results is a
    materially positive signal, and one the headline surprise cannot see. Shade
    upward.

4.  Guidance lowered, withdrawn, or guided to continued decline alongside a
    strong reported quarter is a materially negative signal. Shade downward,
    and do so even when the reported beat was large.

5.  Trailing improvement without forward confirmation does not qualify.
    Sequential margin expansion, cost savings, a narrowing loss, or completed
    destocking are not a turn if the forward outlook still points down.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUALITY OF RESULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.  Non-recurring items are discounted. Earnings inflated by one-time gains,
    tax benefits, asset sales, legal settlements, or inventory revaluations
    receive materially less credit than the same figure from operations. Shade
    downward when a headline result is attributed to items unlikely to repeat.

7.  Per-share figures are less informative than absolute figures. Buybacks
    reduce the share count and can produce per-share growth without
    corresponding business improvement. Where both are available, weight
    absolute revenue, operating income, and net income above per-share
    measures.

8.  Revenue and margin surprises carry information the earnings-per-share
    surprise does not. Published evidence finds top-line and margin surprises
    are only weakly correlated with the headline EPS surprise while having
    comparable economic effect. Where revenue or gross margin diverges from the
    EPS story, weight the divergence heavily — this is precisely the kind of
    information the benchmark cannot capture.

9.  Internal contradiction dampens a positive reaction. Revenue growth
    alongside margin compression, or growth concentrated in a shrinking
    segment, should shade downward relative to a clean result of the same
    headline size.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SMALL-BEAT WARNING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

10. A result that only just clears expectations is treated with suspicion.
    Published evidence finds the usual positive relationship between surprise
    and return weakens or reverses for zero and small positive surprises,
    consistent with the market reading a narrow beat as managed rather than
    earned. Do not reward a narrow beat the way you would reward a decisive
    one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
LANGUAGE AND POSTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

11. Cautionary forward-looking language is a negative signal independent of the
    reported numbers. Hedging about demand, describing a recovery as gradual,
    guiding to the low end of a range, or characterising consensus as
    aggressive should all shade downward.

12. Discount promotional framing. Disclosure summaries are written by the
    company. Superlatives — record, all-time high, best ever — carry no
    ordering information on their own; published and observed results show them
    appearing at both ends of the reaction distribution.

13. Third-party demand evidence outranks internally-generated evidence. Order
    growth, backlog, book-to-bill, renewal rates, and sell-through reflect
    commitments made by customers. Margin expansion and cost reduction reflect
    company action. Weight the former higher when both are present.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
MATERIAL NON-EARNINGS INFORMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

14. Developments outside the period's financial results can dominate them
    entirely: leadership change, restructuring, major contract award or loss,
    litigation, equity issuance or dilution, going-concern language,
    acquisition, or a change in guidance policy. When present, weight these
    above the reported figures.

15. Future-dated promises are discounted relative to delivered change. Total
    addressable market claims, capacity targets years out, letters of intent,
    and roadmap items receive little credit when the current period's core
    metrics are flat or declining.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW HARD TO PRESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

16. These priors are real but individually weak. Any single one of them
    justifies a modest adjustment, not a decisive one. Move confidently only
    when several point the same way and the disclosure states them explicitly.

17. Reserve the extremes. Place an event below 0.15 or above 0.85 only when
    multiple priors above align and are directly supported by the text. Most
    events belong in the middle, and placing them there is correct rather than
    evasive.

18. When the disclosure is thin, or the priors above are silent or point in
    conflicting directions, rank near the middle. A confidently neutral
    placement is the right answer when the text does not support anything
    stronger.
